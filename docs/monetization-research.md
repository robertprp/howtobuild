# HowToBuild monetization research and proposed iterations

Research date: 5 September 2026. **Research and planning only: nothing monetized, no accounts opened, no advertisers contacted, no payment/ad/affiliate integrations added.** Reconfirm program terms before signing. All pilot prices and conversion assumptions below are proposals, not observed HowToBuild results or market guarantees.

## Recommendation

Keep the directory, editorial rankings, guides, and basic copyable starter prompts free. First validate a clearly labeled direct sponsorship with a relevant developer-tool vendor, alongside a small number of genuinely relevant affiliate relationships. Once traffic qualifies, compare a single contextual developer ad placement against the direct sponsor's net return. Explore paid implementation packs before a recurring consumer subscription. A team subscription becomes plausible only if HowToBuild solves an ongoing decision/review problem, not merely the one-time question of which stack to use.

This recommendation is an inference from the product and the programs below. We do not have verified monthly human traffic, geographic distribution, return rate, mailing-list size, sponsor demand, outbound conversion, infrastructure cost, or willingness-to-pay data. The repository's catalog size and GitHub star counts are not audience statistics. Without these inputs, nobody can honestly promise revenue or network acceptance.

## 1. Direct sponsorship: first commercial experiment

Offer a distinct “Sponsored” card, not an editorial recommendation badge or a boosted GitHub ranking. A suitable buyer sells hosting, authentication, observability, developer tooling, or databases to the people reading the relevant guide/category. Do not imply named vendors are interested; no outreach has occurred.

Initial offer to validate: one category/guide placement for four weeks, one approved creative, clear labeling, and an aggregate delivery/click report. A proposed €150–€300 pilot is a pricing interview hypothesis, not a justified rate card without traffic. Define the actual placement, dates, cancellation/make-good terms, and reporting before sale. Never guarantee signups or qualified leads that cannot be measured. Do not sell “sponsored newsletter exposure” before an opted-in newsletter audience exists.

Cooperpress provides a real example of developer-newsletter sponsorship and sponsored content, supporting category fit—not HowToBuild pricing or demand. Its advertising page itself says last updated October 2021, so its audience/open-rate statements are not used as current benchmarks here. [Cooperpress advertising](https://cooperpress.com/advertise/).

Advantages: a single buyer can produce useful revenue at smaller scale than a network, and the placement can be first-party with little layout/performance impact. Disadvantages: finding buyers, contracts, invoicing, creative approval, campaign reporting, and renewals are real labor. An unpaid pilot or vague expression of interest is not willingness-to-pay evidence.

Go/no-go proposal: interview ten relevant vendors using actual audience data, obtain two serious price discussions and one paid pilot after separate approval, then judge renewal intent and net margin after sales time. These counts are internal decision thresholds, not industry standards.

## 2. Affiliates: relevant, modest, and disclosed

DigitalOcean publicly offers 10% of a referred new paying user's monthly spend for one year. Its current page links to Awin while some body copy still mentions CJ; confirm the actual onboarding network, eligibility, attribution window, exclusions, and payout terms directly before joining. Do not assume every provider in our stack selector has an affiliate program. [DigitalOcean program](https://www.digitalocean.com/affiliates), [program agreement](https://www.digitalocean.com/legal/affiliate-program-agreement).

Fit: a hosting comparison or deployment guide can refer someone already deciding what to buy. Weak fit: replacing an otherwise better technology recommendation because a vendor pays commission. Keep free/open-source alternatives equally visible, record editorial reasons independently, and disclose a commercial relationship next to the relevant link.

Illustrative recurring cohort: ten qualifying customers spending $20/month at a 10% commission yield $20/month while all ten remain eligible, at most $240 over twelve months before churn/adjustments. This is not $200/month and not a permanent annuity. Track approved commissions and reversals, not just clicks or account registrations. Confirm whether privacy controls or blocked cookies impair attribution.

Start with a small partner set and useful implementation content. Avoid filling every page with affiliate links, buying traffic before unit economics are known, or recommending weak products to increase commission.

## 3. Developer ad networks: good fit, limited small-site upside

EthicalAds targets developer-focused publishers. Its FAQ normally requires 50,000 monthly pageviews, sometimes making exceptions, and gives roughly $2.50 per 1,000 pageviews for predominantly North American/European traffic. Geography, viewed/paid inventory, blockers, and demand affect results. Its guide requires EthicalAds to be the only ad on the page (own promotions excepted), so do not assume it can coexist with paid direct sponsorships on the same page. [Publisher FAQ](https://www.ethicalads.io/publishers/faq/), [publisher guide](https://www.ethicalads.io/publisher-guide/).

Carbon's FAQ, reviewed 2 September 2026, describes invitation-only selection, developer/designer focus, exclusivity, and a typical $1.20–$2.30 CPM range without guarantees. It distinguishes served impressions from pageviews. Ask for written clarification before combining it with direct sponsors or affiliate placements. No numeric minimum is established by that FAQ; do not invent one. Its current payment terms also involve a delay before earnings are withdrawable, so revenue is not immediate cash. [Carbon FAQ](https://www.carbonads.net/faq).

The two network rates use different denominators and should not be treated as a controlled comparison. Advertiser media-kit CPM is not publisher take-home CPM. Get actual eligible inventory and expected net payout estimates before choosing. A privacy-oriented pitch does not replace a review of the actual client, data flows, and consent requirements.

## 4. Why AdSense is not my first choice

AdSense is an option, but the publisher revenue-share percentage does not predict this site's RPM. Google says content publishers receive 80% after the advertiser-platform fee; buying through Google Ads produces approximately 68% of advertiser spend. Those are shares, not a promise of earnings per page. [AdSense revenue share](https://support.google.com/adsense/answer/180195?hl=en-AU).

Google also imposes certified consent-management requirements for relevant ad serving in the EEA, UK, and Switzerland. That integration, privacy review, layout space, and operational complexity are meaningful costs for a small developer directory. Compare measured revenue and user impact rather than assuming more ad slots make a better business. [Google publisher CMP requirements](https://support.google.com/adsense/answer/13554116?hl=en-GB).

## 5. Paid packs versus a paid subscription

The existing starter prompt is useful but easily copied, and stack selection may be episodic. Charging monthly just to copy it is a weak recurring value proposition. Keep it as a free acquisition/conversion tool.

A stronger one-time product would include a working, maintained vertical slice, explicit versions, setup and deployment instructions, server authorization, a data model, upgrade guidance, and a limited support commitment. ShipFast is an example of a commercial product emphasizing a complete codebase and setup documentation; that is evidence of the product category, not proof that our audience will buy another starter. No competitor price is asserted because the retrieved page did not expose a reliable price. [ShipFast](https://shipfa.st/).

Proposed discovery offer: a €39–€79 narrowly scoped implementation pack, with no unlimited lifetime-support promise. Ask builders what implementation work remains after copying the free prompt, and whether a pack would remove that work. Do not build six stack variants before validating one. Account for maintenance when Next.js/auth/provider interfaces change and for licenses that may restrict redistribution.

For subscriptions, target agencies and small teams: shared stack decisions, architecture decision records, approved-tool policies, dependency/pricing/license-change alerts, and periodic review reports. These are proposed new capabilities, not existing features. Interview at a hypothetical €19–€49/team/month and seek recurring use before billing implementation. Free discovery plus paid workflow/data is a credible pattern: BuiltWith offers free individual lookups and paid intelligence plans, but its enterprise-scale dataset and sales use cases are not comparable to our curated catalog. [BuiltWith plans](https://builtwith.com/plans).

A data API should wait until coverage, freshness, provenance, upstream rights, rate limits, and support justify it. Public GitHub stars alone are unlikely to be sufficient differentiation. A paid job board would add a different product and is not justified merely because the visual reference was a jobs site.

## Illustrative economics—not forecasts

| Scenario                            | Arithmetic                                                    | Gross revenue before costs |
| ----------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------- |
| EthicalAds-like pageview model      | 50,000 pageviews × $2.50 / 1,000                              | $125/month                 |
| Same model at higher scale          | 100,000 × $2.50 / 1,000                                       | $250/month                 |
| Carbon-like served-impression model | 50,000 paid/eligible served impressions × $1.20–$2.30 / 1,000 | $60–$115/month             |
| One direct pilot                    | 1 placement × assumed €250                                    | €250 for the pilot period  |
| Small implementation-pack cohort    | 10 orders × assumed €49                                       | €490 one-time              |
| Small team cohort                   | 20 teams × assumed €29/month                                  | €580 MRR                   |

The ad rows are arithmetic using provider statements, not guaranteed accepted/filled inventory. The euro rows are our hypotheses, not external benchmarks. They are not currency-converted or necessarily additive: exclusivity can prohibit mixing inventory. At $2.50 per thousand pageviews, $1,000 gross requires roughly 400,000 monetizing pageviews under that simplified model. This is why ads alone should not drive the early roadmap.

Measure contribution margin after hosting, email, support/editorial time, sales time, refunds, processing, and tax obligations. Example hypothesis: a €250 sponsor less six hours at an internal €30/hour costs €180 in labor, leaving €70 before other costs. Twenty paying teams is not equivalent to profitable recurring revenue if each needs substantial support. Model churn: at 20 accounts and an assumed 5% monthly logo churn, approximately one replacement customer per month is needed just to remain flat.

Do not select payment infrastructure yet. For eventual digital products/subscriptions, compare standard processing plus your own tax/billing work with a merchant of record. Lemon Squeezy's fee documentation shows a 5% + $0.50 base in its example and additional international, PayPal, subscription, and payout fees in applicable cases; advertised base fees are not the full cost. Merchant-of-record handling does not eliminate your own business/accounting obligations. Eligibility and product approval still need review. [Lemon Squeezy fees](https://docs.lemonsqueezy.com/help/getting-started/fees), [merchant-of-record offering](https://www.lemonsqueezy.com/pricing).

## Trust, SEO, and privacy constraints

1. Sell clearly identified exposure or a separate product, never an editorial recommendation, moderation approval, favorable review, or automatic trending position.
2. Mark paid links with `rel="sponsored"`; keep editorial ranking logic independent. Google specifically recommends that attribute for ads/paid placements. [Google outbound-link guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/qualify-outbound-links).
3. Make sponsorship/affiliate relationships evident next to the placement rather than only in a footer. Relevant material-connection guidance is published by the FTC; applicability depends on audience and jurisdiction. [FTC endorsement guidance](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/ftcs-endorsement-guides-what-people-are-asking).
4. If operating in Spain or reaching regulated audiences, have the actual cookies/tracking, notices, newsletter consent, and withdrawal mechanisms reviewed. Do not assume “contextual” means every legal requirement disappears. The AEPD's retrieved cookie guide is dated May 2024; confirm applicable updates before launch. [AEPD cookie guide](https://www.aepd.es/guias/guia-cookies.pdf). This report is product research, not legal advice.
5. Do not sell user/session data or silently enroll contributors in a sponsored newsletter. Do not add ads to auth, account, admin, or error pages. Preserve page speed, accessible labels, and a clear distinction between ads and tool cards.

## Proposed post-launch monetization iterations (not implemented)

These can become Phase 7+ after the Phase 6 scope is confirmed. Dates below are suggested experiment durations, not promises of approval or results.

**M1 — Evidence and interviews, two weeks.** Gather 30 days of real human audience data if available; otherwise wait for a meaningful baseline. Interview ten vendors and ten builders with consent. Record actual pains, objections, audience fit, and price responses. Output: one monetization model to pilot, no checkout/ad script.

**M2 — Sponsor/affiliate pilot, four to six weeks, only after approval.** One labeled sponsor placement or a small relevant affiliate pilot with approved disclosures and attribution. Define make-good terms, ownership, and net-margin reporting. Stop if it damages trust, cannot deliver promised exposure, or costs more in servicing than it earns. Judge qualified click/conversion evidence and renewal interest, not vanity impressions.

**M3 — Paid product discovery.** Choose either a narrow pack or an ongoing team decision workflow based on interviews; do not build both. Proposed gate: five explicit price commitments and repeated use of a manual prototype, followed by separate approval to accept payments. Define support limits, refunds, tax treatment, licenses, and maintenance before sale. No fake scarcity or unavailable purchase flow.

**M4 — Network comparison at sufficient scale.** Once eligible, request current terms/forecasts from EthicalAds and Carbon, check exclusivity, then compare one compliant placement against direct-sponsor economics. Report net revenue per eligible human session, implementation cost, page performance, retention, and editorial impact. Do not add network estimates to sponsor revenue when contracts conflict.

**M5 — Expand only a validated winner.** Invest in subscriptions only when actual repeated workflow use and retention justify it. Prioritize sponsor renewals, useful paid deliverables, and positive contribution margin over adding more monetization surfaces.

## What the owner needs to supply

Monthly human pageviews/visitors, geography mix, organic queries, return frequency, existing mailing-list consent/size, current costs, hours available for sales/support, business country/entity, and desired monthly profit. Confirm whether preserving a completely ad-free UI is a priority. Without these, the recommendation is a ranked starting hypothesis rather than a financial forecast.

Bottom line: **sell relevant reach first, implementation value second, and recurring workflow value only when it exists. Keep the trusted discovery layer free.**
